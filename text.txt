What is public housing in Hong Kong?

Public housing in Hong Kong is funded by the government housing programme through which the government of Hong Kong provides affordable housing for lower-income residents, who cannot afford to rent privately. 


Landscape and development of Hong Kong: how it all started?

Hong Kong’s urban landscape has a distinctive architectural visual. The region’s mountainous landscape and limited developable land have made it hard to build the urban areas horizontally, which contributes to the city's vertical growth. The region developed from a small military and trading area, into a major international city during its colonial years. Under British colonial rule, bordering mainland China, population grew considerably, the region being a centre of trade and businesses. Over the years people migrated from the mainland in search of economic opportunities, while some others fled political pressure. As more people arrived in Hong Kong, the region faced increasing pressure to accommodate the growing population within limited geographically developable land. 

During devastating fires in the 1950s, the government began building public housing on a large scale. This programme grew rapidly. By 1965 nearly one-third of Hong Kong’s population was living in public housing. From the 1960s the government began developing the outskirts of the city, combining residential areas with employment opportunities, particularly in manufacturing. Rapid population growth raised a concern about limited public housing, which influenced government housing policies during the colonial period. But changes in the eligibility rules for the accommodation favoured certain groups while excluding others. This could demonstrate that public housing changes were not only a response to the shortage of living space, but also a political issue involving social identity and perception of fairness. 

As any region’s population grows and the demand for housing increases, it is natural for urban development to expand in order to accommodate more residents and support economic activity. However, it is expectable for this process to become more complicated, when geographical landscape limits the actual available land for development. This puts pressure on the ability to accommodate each resident in a limited space. Hong Kong illustrates an extreme example of the challenge, where residents struggle to find housing both affordable and adequate in size in society, where they have to come to terms with notoriously high housing prices. 
Hong Kong’s property prices are influenced by several factors. Research papers confirm, demographic changes increase the demand for housing, while the expansion of businesses with mainland China and the West, the financial sector, created a demand for commercial property. As demand grew faster than the supply of properties, prices rose rapidly. Subsequently housing affordability became an increasing issue during the 1980s and 1990s. And additionally, home ownership led to a growing mortgage debt. 

High demand and limited housing availability takes a toll on the sustainable accommodation, even though verticality and high-density developments allow Hong Kong to accommodate a large population within a relatively small area, it is still not enough. Certain measures have to be taken to distribute the living space. But also, are offered standards for living enough for expanding the family, changing the job or majorly changing personal life?

After the initial first impression acquired from the media, I began researching the topic through studies, articles and what stood out to me this time too, same as with initial research through media, was poverty and inequality in Hong Kong. Housing difficulties in Hong Kong primarily affect poorer and socially disadvantaged people, reducing their disposable income and limiting their daily and social activities. For many residents finding affordable housing is a major challenge forcing people to live in extremely small spaces and sacrificing basic needs, safety of people, and hygiene conditions. Difficulties accessing affordable public housing pushes vulnerable residents of Hong Kong into accommodations that may not provide adequate living standards. Housing is not just the matter of having a roof over the head, it does affect people’s financial security, independence and growing, well-being, opportunities in life, participation in society and Hong Kong residents who face those difficulties spread across groups of low-income families, students, young adults and adults chasing their independence, elderly residents and also those, who actually provide public rental housings. 
Regarding the renters, many rental buildings were constructed during the property boom during 1950-1960s. Overtime, the poor maintenance, lack of effective management, and unauthorized structural extensions contributed to deterioration of many properties. Poorly maintained buildings present serious safety risks to residents living in those, including dangers of fire and structural failures. But maintaining older buildings places a financial burden on the owners, who are required to pay for repairs. This is particularly difficult to elderly property owners who rely on the income coming from these residential buildings.
As mentioned above the gap between housing cost and affordability affects young single adults too. In one older study, 2009, it was found that 81% of single applicants for public housing, most of whom were under the age of 30, applied because they wanted to live independently. Young adults seek affordable housing to establish their independence, but limited access to affordable housing can therefore delay their ability to move out of the family home, slowing down the process of achieving greater independence early on. The lack of long-term planning by the government to accommodate the growing number of single-adults contributes to the pressure of Hong Kong’s public housing system.
According to government figures from the article, reported in 2023, general applicants wait an average of 5.3 years to be housed. Many applicants are left waiting for years for suitable accommodation, and meanwhile many of them turn to subdivided flats for help, to find temporary accommodation. But many temporary accommodations being older buildings with unsafe conditions brings us back to the topic of  moral fairness and basic human rights to have a safe livable space. Do people have to wait for over 5 years to acquire an affordable comfortable accommodation instead of a tiny home with no private shower or kitchen with unsanitary conditions? 

Conclusion

Access to housing is a fundamental human right, I believe, every person should be able to have a safe place to live, as well as access to basic necessities. It was this perspective of mine that made me want to understand how a city known for economic success and urban development combines an extreme poverty within it, facing serious housing challenges and making media outlets primarily showcase challenges that vulnerable people have to face everyday. 
Answering to the question of how high-density urban development has shaped the everyday lives of Hong Kongers I can summarise, that although vertical development of the region, land reclamation and public housing have helped accommodate a growing population within limited space and time, housing affordability and adequate conditions remain as biggest concerns and challenges of Hong Kongers and the government. Difficulties to accommodate people adequately affects several groups of population. A city’s economical success does not ensure safe and affordable living for the residents, the government's concern must be to provide decent life for people, who call it home.
